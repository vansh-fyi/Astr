<DocSection id="shared" title="Shared rules">

Both gradients take their colour from `--grad-color`, which accepts any colour stop, and their direction from `--grad-dir` where it applies. Every alpha is one of the `--mag-*` steps, and interpolation happens in sRGB so a Flutter gradient renders identically.

A computed alpha is snapped to the nearest step:

$$ n^{*} = \operatorname{clamp}\bigl(\operatorname{round}(-5\log_{10}\alpha),\,0,\,10\bigr) $$

Beyond the last step there is a floor: when $-5\log_{10}\alpha \ge 10.5$ the stop is fully transparent instead of `--mag-10`.

</DocSection>

<DocSection id="extinction" title="Atmospheric extinction">

Light from a star loses brightness as it crosses more atmosphere. The loss in magnitudes is proportional to the relative airmass $X$ minus one, with an extinction coefficient $k$:

$$ \alpha(z) = 10^{-0.4\,k\,(X(z)-1)},\qquad k = 0.2 $$

The airmass for a zenith angle $z$ in degrees comes from the Kasten and Young approximation:

$$ X(z) = \frac{1}{\cos z + 0.50572\,\left(96.07995^{\circ} - z\right)^{-1.6364}} $$

Stops sit at z = 0, 60, 70, 78, 84, 88 and 90 degrees. Each stop's position along the gradient is $p = z / 90^{\circ}$, so the zenith is at 0% and the horizon at 100%.

<DocSection id="moffat" title="Moffat profile">

A point source blurred by the atmosphere spreads into a glow described by the Moffat function of radius $r$ and width $a$:

$$ I(r) = \left(1 + (r/a)^2\right)^{-\beta},\qquad \beta = 3 $$

The gradient reaches full transparency at a radius of four widths:

$$ R = 4a $$

so a stop at position $p$ sits at $r = 4a\,p$, where the position is measured as $p = r / (4a)$.
